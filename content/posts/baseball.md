## Introduction
I have a bizarre fascination with reading about the death of baseball. [Fans](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskAnAmerican/comments/15oku93/is_it_baseball_dying/), [pundits](https://web.archive.org/web/20220406092659/https://www.nytimes.com/2022/04/06/opinion/baseball-nationalize.html), and [media](https://www.vice.com/en/article/why-does-anyone-watch-baseball/) alike all bring their own flavor of discussion to the table, but a few agreed upon facts have emerged; baseball is less popular and less watched than 20 years ago, and yet while paradoxically overall team value has persisted (and hence the future is not totally bleak), it feels like there's a real and pervasive anxiety about where things are headed and what the solutions may be.

Many ardent baseball fans have lobbied for baseball online and covered familiar ground. Possibly missing from this though are the perspectives from the noob fans, which is unfortunate -- After all, it may be possible that this segment of fans contains fresh answers to baseball's decline and where it'll go from here. I figured I'd set out on an attempt to articulate why baseball may be interesting to those who do not watch it all as a noob fan myself since this year. 

Note that given I have only watched baseball under it's current ruleset, I have no context on what it was like when MLB allowed doping, nor what the game was like prior to big changes such as the pitch clock. 

## Baseball Is Slow, But That's Okay

It is certainly true that baseball is slower paced than other sports. There's just a lot more standing around involved as a consequence of the game having events that are very discrete in nature (I think this is baseball's biggest flaw as a sport and possibly it's biggest strength as a *game*). This definitely bothered me as a first time viewer, but I think it's also not really as big of a deal as some may expect -- Other sports also have effectively dead time where nothing important is happening (at least to the perception of a noob fan), and slower pace of play results in a more versatile viewing experience as both something to watch passively as well as upping the tension during critical moments. 

The real issue with baseball's slower pace is more likely that it is not flashy for a traditional sport, so there is less obvious payoff to the noob fan. In this way, baseball is less like a sport and more like watching two teams play a slightly contrived version of chess. Maybe the most apt comparison will be to [chessboxing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OqWmV6H0uTA).

I think this may be why many people online suggest watching baseball with someone who can explain the finer points of the game, though clearly that's not always possible. It really is not obvious how cerebral the game as a first time viewer. What becomes clear on watching multiple games is how you realize that everything has an aspect of strategy, from the batting order to the pitch sequence chosen to where the outfielders choose to stand against certain hitters. That's not even to say the more macro aspect of things, such as how some players are rotated in and out of a game due to their respective specialities, which is an intented and understood aspect of the baseball meta. In fact, a subset of players on every team will specifically be for standing in for the starting line up as the game progresses, and so both the choice of player and the exact timing of when this should happen are both free variables. This is also not even to mention the more obvious example of sabermetrics/baseball statistics, which is shockingly mature and has substantially more weight assigned to it compared to other sports (again, this is possible as a result of being a more discrete game).
